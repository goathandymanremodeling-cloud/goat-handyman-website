Frequently Asked Questions

The cost depends on the amount of work, material choices, existing conditions, access, and the preparation needed before installation or finishing. Demolition, disposal, hidden damage, and specialist work can also affect the total. A useful estimate should describe what is included rather than relying only on a room size, photo, or advertised starting price.

A written estimate should identify the work, materials or allowances, price, and expected schedule. It should also clarify preparation, disposal, finishing, and exclusions so both parties understand the scope. Before work begins, the contract should explain payment terms and how additional work will be approved. Clear details help prevent disagreements about what the price covers.

Contractors may be pricing different work even when the project description sounds similar. One estimate may include demolition, surface preparation, materials, painting, and cleanup, while another leaves some of those items out. Compare the written scope, product specifications, exclusions, and warranty terms before comparing totals. Ask for clarification when a price difference is substantial.

Usually, it is better to confirm the project scope, measurements, and product requirements first. A door, vanity, fixture, or flooring product may look suitable but require additional modifications to fit. If you want to supply materials, agree on compatibility, quantities, delivery timing, and responsibility for returns or missing parts before placing the order.
